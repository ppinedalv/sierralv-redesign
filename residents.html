How to maintain your home

Try these first.

Many common problems have a two-minute fix. Check these before submitting a request — it could save you waiting for a service appointment.

No power
  • Check the breaker panel (usually in a closet or on the side of the house) for a tripped switch.
  • Reset it by moving it fully to "off," then back to "on."
  • Unplug everything, press the reset button on the GFCI outlet, then plug appliances back in one at a time to find the faulty one.
No hot water
  • Check whether the water heater tank is leaking.
  • For a gas water heater, check whether the pilot light is out. If you can't relight it, call your gas provider for help.
Garbage disposal not working
  • Press the reset button on the bottom of the unit under the sink.
  • With the switch off, check for blockage around the blades.
  • Never put chicken bones, onion peels, potato skins, carrots or other firm items down the disposal.
Clogged or slow sink

Skip harsh chemical cleaners. Pour a pot of boiling water down the drain, add ½ cup baking soda, then 1 cup white vinegar mixed with 1 cup hot water. Let it sit 5–10 minutes and flush again with boiling water. For tubs and showers, boiling water alone often clears hair and soap build-up.

Clogged toilet
  • Use a plunger with short, quick motions.
  • Pouring a full bucket of water into the bowl can create enough force to clear it.
Washing machine not working
  • Make sure both water taps behind the machine are fully on.
  • Check the lid is fully closed and the dial is set directly on a cycle.
  • Don't overload it — that can stop a wash halfway.
  • Use only detergent made for your machine (never dish soap).
Dryer not working

Dryers can shut off automatically when overloaded or overheating. Clean the lint filter after every load — a clogged filter is the leading cause of dryer fires.

Refrigerator not cooling
  • Check the thermostat setting inside.
  • Make sure food or ice isn't blocking the vents at the back of the freezer.
  • Vacuum the coils under or behind the fridge.
  • Keep door seals clean so cold air doesn't escape.

Still not working? Submit a maintenance request or call 702-553-1211.

Applicant & tenant information

Fair housing, for everyone.

The federal Fair Housing Act — enforced by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) — and Nevada's own fair housing laws, overseen by the Nevada Equal Rights Commission (NERC), protect your right to equal access to housing.

Housing providers must also make reasonable accommodations for people with disabilities, including permitting service animals and adjusting rules or policies when needed.

If you believe you've experienced discrimination, you can file a complaint with HUD (federal law) or NERC (state law).

HUD Fair Housing

Protected classes

  • Race
  • Color
  • Religion
  • Sex, including sexual orientation and gender identity
  • Disability
  • Familial status — families with children under 18 and pregnant women
  • National origin
